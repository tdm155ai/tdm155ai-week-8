**TDM 155AI · Unit 2 · Week 8, October 28 · The First Project is 30% of your grade**

Across October you chose a form, studied it, practiced its moves, and edited a piece. Two things are left: finish the piece and gather everything about how you made it by **Friday, November 6**, then talk about it on the podcast the following week.

## Due Friday, November 6

### 1. Your finished piece

The thing you set out to make in your form, finished: something you would show someone outside the class.

### 2. Your context repository

A **private GitHub repository or Google Drive folder** holding anything and everything that helps someone understand the making of your piece. Put in whatever you'd like us to see. For example:

- your inspiration board, references, and the examples you studied, with your notes on them
- your notebook from week 5: research, how-tos, readings, dictated and handwritten notes
- your studies and practice attempts from week 6, including the ones that didn't work
- raw material, drafts, alternate cuts, and the edit as it changed
- prompts, chats, and AI output you used, and what you did with them
- your weekly Slack posts and reflection videos from October
- credits: sources, collaborators, and any music, footage, or images you didn't make

Organize it so a person or an AI assistant can find their way around. A short README at the top saying what's there and where to start will help a lot.

**Who reads it.** Share it with Marlon and Madeleine, who will review it, and with your podcast partners, who will use it to prepare to interview you. The better it shows your process, the better the questions you'll get.

**This is a dry run of the final portfolio** due December 11, which is what you're graded on for the course. Use it to practice gathering and presenting your work.\*

## The podcast: the week after

**Schedule one hour with two classmates.** In that hour you record three 15-minute interviews, one about each person's piece, rotating roles so that each of you takes every role once:

| Round | Guest | Host | Producer / sidekick |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A | B | C |
| 2 | B | C | A |
| 3 | C | A | B |

The goal of every interview is to get at the guest's best thinking about what they made.

- **Guest.** Talk about your intentions, your process, what you learned, and the choices you made. Say what you can do now that you couldn't in September, and what the tools contributed compared with what you contributed.
- **Host.** Before the hour, watch or listen to the guest's piece and go through their context repository. Prepare questions. You can use AI to help you prepare from their repository, but check what it tells you against the work itself. In the interview, listen and follow the thread.
- **Producer / sidekick.** Watch the time and keep the conversation on track. Notice the thread the host might miss, ask the basic question, push back on a claim, and help the host get to the guest's best answer.

**How it's assessed.** We consider both the work and your account of making it: your engagement with the form, the skills you developed, your creative decisions, and your ability to explain what you and your tools contributed. Your context repository and the podcast are where you show it.

\* In your final portfolio, due Friday, December 11, you can add further reflections on this project, and you can revise and augment your earlier work. Note what you changed.
